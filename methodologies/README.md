# Methodologies — the Fifteen Engines

> Purpose: index the fifteen decision engines, so the right procedure is found by the question it answers rather than by its number.
> Read when: you know a narrative decision is missing but not which one; or a workflow names an engine and you need its file.
> Links: `SKILL.md` — the doctrine and the eight decisions these engines implement. `references/narrative-theory.md` — the concepts the procedures operationalise. `workflows/` — the fourteen routes that call these engines in order. `templates/decision-ledger.md` — where the outputs are recorded.

## How to use the index

Each engine follows the same eight-part format — purpose, when and when not, inputs, numbered procedure, failure criteria, typical errors, output format, micro-example — and each file ends with a section on how its engines combine. Read the procedure and the failure list; skip the rest on a first pass. The engines are procedures, not chapters: two or three per task is the working number, and a story that satisfies all fifteen is a story with no centre.

The fastest way in is the failure list. Read the criteria section of two or three engines and pick the one whose failures match the symptom in front of you; the procedure then tells you how to repair it. Starting from the procedure instead invites running an engine whose question the story does not need.

Engines are reusable across lengths; what changes is resolution. E2 on a 1,200-word story is five nodes and one counterfactual; on a novel it is a spine per act. Do not scale an engine by filling in more of its output format — scale it by asking its question at the size of the work.

## Combinations that work

- **E1 → E2 → E6.** Premise, chain, information. Each supplies the next one's input: the ending must answer E1's question, and E6's final revelation must land at or after the decision E1 named. This is the planning spine for anything above a scene.
- **E3 + E8.** Motivation supplies the agenda; the scene spends it. Running E8 without E3 produces scenes where someone wants something for no stated reason; running E3 without E8 produces a character file that never reaches the page.
- **E6 + E7.** Order is the cheapest revelation mechanism: whether the reader learns a fact before or after a scene is a decision about what the scene means. A braid is a plant-and-payoff schedule with dates attached.
- **E4 + E9.** A contradiction becomes audible in what a character will not say. E4 finds the cost; E9 finds the evasion that carries it in dialogue without stating it.
- **E13 + E14.** The ledger supplies the critique's evidence, and the critique ranks which ledger hit is load-bearing. Without E13 the diagnosis guesses; without E14 the ledger never becomes a plan.
- **E11 + E8.** The modal test belongs to E11 but is run on the scene's turn, before the prose exists. Running it on finished sentences produces adjective-swapping, which is the most common wasted revision.
- **E5 + E15.** Relationship asymmetry is what makes an unreliable account legible: the narrator bends the truth toward someone, and the reader locates the bend by watching who is being managed.

## Do not stack

The failure of over-application is its own signature: cautious, symmetrical, thesis-driven prose that passes every gate and moves nobody. On anything under roughly 2,000 words, run at most three engines at full resolution `[hypothesis]`; the rest are checks, not procedures.

Four stacks over-constrain in specific ways.

- **E1 + E2 + E6 + E7 + E8 + E11 + E14 on a short story.** Seven gates, one text, no centre. Each engine is defensible alone; together they force the draft to satisfy decisions the story does not need, and the result reads as an outline with scenes attached.
- **E6 + E15 on a short piece.** Two mystery systems compete: the reader cannot tell whether a gap is withheld information or a bent narrator, and both readings are half-supported. Choose which one the story is about.
- **E7 + E8 on a first draft.** Braided structure built from scenes that do not turn is a more expensive way to be flat. Fix the scene, then decide the order.
- **E8's gate + E14's triage on a draft that has no effect yet.** Revising before the draft has done anything to a reader produces repairs against a text nobody has experienced. Write to the end, then diagnose.

Two rules keep the stack honest. If two engines disagree about the same scene, decide which one the scene is for and record the choice; do not split the difference. If a third engine is added to fix a problem the first two did not find, the problem is usually at a higher level — go up to E1 or E2, not sideways to another checklist.

## What no engine does

No engine produces prose. They produce decisions, and a decision executed in flat sentences is invisible; E10 and the prose files are where the decision becomes perceptible. No engine scores quality: there is no number in this directory, and any procedure that produced one would reward balance over character. And no engine replaces a reader. The failure lists catch the errors that have names; the unnamed error — the scene that is correct and dead — is caught only by someone who wanted to keep reading.
