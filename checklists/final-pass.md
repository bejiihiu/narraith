# Final pass

> Fourteen checks on a draft before it leaves your hands. Ordered by leverage: do not start at the bottom.

## Structure

- [ ] **The question survives.** Restate the opening's dramatic question in one line, then check that the middle has not quietly replaced it. *If it fails:* the draft is answering a question the reader was not asked. Cut the material that belongs to the other question, or rewrite the opening so it asks the one the book actually answers.

- [ ] **The ending answers that question, at a cost.** *If it fails:* an answer obtained free is a resolution, not an ending. Move the cost into the final movement — the last choice should take something the character wanted at the start.

- [ ] **The protagonist pays.** The person whose POV carries the book is the person who loses the most. *If it fails:* you are watching someone else's story from a convenient seat. Move the POV, or move the cost.

- [ ] **Every scene changes a state.** Spot-check the three you suspect most. *If it fails:* a scene that changes nothing is summary. Merge its one useful beat into a neighbour and cut the rest.

- [ ] **Every turn is caused by a choice.** Check the two biggest turns, not all of them. *If it fails:* the turn is an arrival, an accident or a message. Move the turn onto a decision the character makes knowing the price.

- [ ] **Information order.** Nothing is revealed before the reader can feel its cost, and nothing is withheld past the point of guessing. *If it fails:* move the revelation one scene later, or move the fact that gives it weight one scene earlier — the second is usually cheaper.

- [ ] **Subplots touch the spine twice.** Once to open, once to pay. *If it fails:* a subplot that only varies tone is a digression. Cut it, or connect it at a second point where it changes the main line's stakes.

## Scene shape

- [ ] **Read the last line of every scene.** In sequence, out loud. Does each land on the change? *If it fails:* the scene ends after its turn. Cut back to the line where the change happens.

- [ ] **Entry points.** Every scene starts after the ritual and inside pressure. *If it fails:* the first paragraph is setup. Delete it and start at the first line that costs something.

- [ ] **POV and distance hold.** One head per scene unless the shift is deliberate and marked. *If it fails:* head-hopping — a judgement from outside the head inside an inside view. Rewrite the outliers to the scene's distance.

## Voice and originality

- [ ] **Read 300 words aloud.** Is this a narrator, or a camera? *If it fails:* [L3] is flat. Establish the narrator's attitude in the first three sentences and remove the prose any competent narrator could have written.

- [ ] **Modal test on the opening and the climax.** Given the same setup, would a competent re-writer produce these? *If it fails:* both are the default and no amount of line work will fix it. Change one decision at each and rewrite outward from the change.

- [ ] **The refusal is visible.** The alternative you rejected at the pre-draft stage is still legible in the text — the story shows it considered and declined. *If it fails:* the draft reads as the only option, which is what "predictable" means. Let one character name the obvious solution and let the story refuse it for a reason.

## Delivery

- [ ] **Continuity and language.** Run `checklists/continuity-sweep.md`, then the prose checklist for the language: `checklists/russian-prose.md` or `checklists/english-prose.md`. *If it fails:* a contradiction or a translationese pattern has survived to here. Fix contradictions before line work — a polished sentence does not survive a broken fact.

## If you have ten minutes

Three checks catch the defects that make the others moot: the question survives, the last line of every scene, the modal test on the opening. Run those and stop. A fourteenth-pass polish of a scene that fails check 8 is wasted work.

## What this list cannot see

- Whether the story is worth telling. It checks execution, not the decision to write it.
- Whether the genre contract is met — that is `references/genre-and-form.md`.
- Whether the sample was long enough to support the verdict — that is `references/literary-critique.md`, §8.

## Then stop

- Do not add a fifteenth check. A draft that passes fourteen and still feels wrong needs a diagnosis (`references/literary-critique.md`), not another list.
- Do not run this list on a 200-word sample. The structural checks cannot see structure at that length.
- Do not report the checklist to the user. The pass happened; the artifact stays behind.
