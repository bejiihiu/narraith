# Scene gate

> Run on every scene, in order. Stop at the first failure, fix it, then re-run from the top — a fix at gate 3 often reopens gate 1.

## The six gates

- [ ] **State change.** Name what is different at the end: knowledge, trust, power, possibility, or the reader's understanding. *If it fails:* the scene is a fragment. Fold it into a scene that changes something, or give it a change and cut whatever the change made redundant. *The one exception:* a scene built on a deliberate refusal to move a value. Then the refusal must be marked and paid, and the text must know it is a refusal.

- [ ] **Agency.** Name who acts and what the action costs them. *If it fails:* the turn is produced by an accident, a message, or a new character arriving. Move the turn onto a decision — even a bad one — that the character makes knowing the price. *The exception:* when the subject is the impossibility of choice itself, passivity must be chosen against visible alternatives and priced on the page; keep the turn and name the constraint.

- [ ] **Entry and exit.** Enter after the ritual, leave before the farewell. *If it fails:* the scene opens with greeting or setup and closes with settling. Cut to the first line that has pressure, and end on the change rather than after it.

- [ ] **Information.** Name the thing the reader now holds: a detail, a discrepancy, a question. *If it fails:* the scene only transmits plot. Add one concrete thing the reader can misread, or one detail that will mean something else later.

- [ ] **Voice.** Name what the narration does that only this narrator could do — an attitude, a lexical field, a habit of attention. *If it fails:* [L3] is flat. Establish the narrator's attitude in the first three sentences and remove prose any competent narrator could have written.

- [ ] **Modal test.** Given the same setup, would a competent re-writer produce this scene? *If it fails:* you wrote the default. Change the decision, not the adjectives — pick the alternative you rejected at the pre-draft stage and follow it for one scene.

## Six more specific checks

- [ ] **Cost is on the page.** The price of the choice is dramatised, not asserted in the next scene's summary. *If it fails:* render the payment at the distance where the reader can see what it takes from the character, or move the consequence into this scene.

- [ ] **Local stakes.** The character has something to lose *in this scene*, not only in the book's arc. *If it fails:* the scene runs on borrowed tension. Give the character a small thing they want from this room, and make it reachable.

- [ ] **Distance holds.** POV distance is constant except at a deliberate, marked shift. *If it fails:* you are head-hopping — the camera is inside one head for the observation and outside it for the judgement. Pick the distance the scene needs and rewrite the outliers to it.

- [ ] **Channel by function.** Emotion and description arrive through the channel the scene needs, not through reflex. *If it fails:* every feeling is a physical sensation and every room is seen. Choose one non-visual sense for this scene, or let the character's attention carry the feeling instead of their body.

- [ ] **No furniture characters.** Everyone present wants something, or leaves. *If it fails:* you have a listener who exists to be told things. Give them an agenda that interferes, or cut them and let the protagonist think.

- [ ] **Deletion test.** Remove the scene and name the two downstream things that break. *If it fails:* fewer than two means the scene is load-bearing for nothing. Merge its one useful beat into a neighbour and cut the rest.

## Reading this list

The order is not arbitrary. Gates 1–2 decide whether the scene is a scene; gates 3–6 decide whether it works as one. Running 5 before 1 polishes a fragment.

## Worked failure

A scene: a letter arrives revealing the husband's affair; the wife reads it; the chapter ends.

- Gate 1 passes: she knows now, and the reader does too.
- Gate 2 fails: the turn is the letter. Nobody chose anything.
- The tempting repair is to make the letter more shocking. That is gate 2 failing again, with better adjectives.
- The actual repair: the letter arrives before the scene opens. The scene's turn is now her decision — to answer his question about dinner as if nothing had happened, in a voice she has to construct.
- Gate 4 now passes: the reader holds the gap between what she knows and what she says.
- Gate 6: the modal scene is "she reads it and reacts". The non-modal one is "she reads it, then does something ordinary with terrible precision". The second is where the story is.

## What the gate cannot see

- **Structure.** A scene can pass all twelve and belong in the wrong place, or be the third scene doing the same job.
- **Pacing.** A scene can pass and still arrive after the reader has stopped caring.
- **Continuity.** Nothing here checks whether the letter's date matches chapter two.
- Those belong to `checklists/final-pass.md`, `checklists/continuity-sweep.md` and the structural pass in `references/editorial-intelligence.md`.

## When the gate fails twice

- Two failures at gates 1–2 mean the scene's job is wrong, not its execution. Restate the job in one sentence before rewriting.
- Two failures at gates 3–6 with gates 1–2 passing mean the scene works and reads badly. That is a line pass, not a rewrite.
- A scene that fails the modal test and passes everything else is the most common draft scene: competent, correct, replaceable. Change one decision at the setup and rewrite from there.
