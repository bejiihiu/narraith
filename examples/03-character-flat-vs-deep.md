# Firing someone you hired — flat character vs. a character who pays

> Purpose: show the difference between a character described by traits and a character defined by a want, a need and a lie that cost her something on the page.
> Read when: a character scene reads competent and weightless, or when a draft explains a motive in one sentence and expects the reader to accept it.
> Related: `references/character-intelligence.md` — the three axes, contradiction types and the agency audit used here; `references/quality-levels.md` — L5/L6 symptoms and their misattribution; `methodologies/02-character-and-dialogue.md` — engine E3, the procedure this example instantiates.

## What this example tests

A character whose interiority is reported rather than priced. The scene turns on a decision that changes nothing for her: she keeps her job, her self-image and her relief, and the prose hands her absolution in the last line. The failure sits at [L6] — motivation explained, contradiction absent — and surfaces at [L5] as a turn that costs nobody anything.

## Setup

Nadia runs the customer-service floor of a regional heating-parts distributor. A reduction list arrives from the regional office with one name on it: Ellis, a dispatcher she hired three years ago. She has one conversation to have, and a folder prepared for it.

## Weak version

**Weak.**

Nadia had always been efficient and fair, and she prided herself on both. So when the regional office sent down the reduction list and hers was the shortest in the division — one name — she did not put it off. Ellis had been hers to hire three years ago. He was the best dispatcher on the floor, quick with the drivers and patient with the customers who rang up to shout, and that made the conversation harder without making it complicated.

She booked the small meeting room for four o'clock, the quiet hour, and laid out the folder: the dates, the severance figure, the letter from HR that explained the reasoning in plain language. When he came in she did not make him wait.

"The volumes are down eleven percent since spring," she said. "The company can't carry a full floor on these numbers. It isn't about your work."

He asked whether it was about the March error, when he had put two loads on the same lorry and the customer had gone elsewhere. She said it wasn't; the error had been fixed and the account had come back. He nodded, and thanked her for telling him straight, and asked whether he could finish the week.

She said yes. After he left she sat for a minute with the folder closed, feeling the weight of it, and then she went back out and took three calls herself, and by six the floor was running normally. She was sorry about Ellis. She was also relieved, and she knew which of the two she would be paid for.

## Diagnosis

| Symptom | Level | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The character is introduced as an adjective pair | L6 | A trait list is a description, not a force; nothing in the scene can block "efficient" or "fair" | "efficient and fair, and she prided herself on both" |
| The motive is complete and correct | L6 | The stated reason closes the inference the reader was supposed to perform; no displaced motive, no gap between reason and cause | "The volumes are down eleven percent since spring" |
| The turn costs her nothing | L5 | No option is priced. She keeps the job, the decision, and the good opinion of everyone in the room | "He nodded, and thanked her for telling him straight" |
| Efficiency and fairness never collide | L6 | There is no contradiction because the two traits are compatible; nothing forces a choice between goods she actually holds | "that made the conversation harder without making it complicated" |
| Any competent manager could occupy the scene | L6 | Substitutability: swap her for a colleague and every line survives, so the choice belongs to the situation | the whole exchange, including the rehearsed folder |
| The last line assigns the meaning | L4 | The narrator names the moral balance the reader should have assembled | "she knew which of the two she would be paid for" |

## Strong version

**Strong.**

Nadia kept the sheet folded twice in the inside pocket of her bag. She took it out on the platform at seven-forty, read it while the train came in, and put it back without folding it again, which was unlike her.

The reduction was one name. Ellis.

She had hired him three years ago, in the spring the floor went from six to nine, and he had turned out better at the rota than she was. That was not why his name was on the sheet. His name was on the sheet because of a column four rows down in her own handwriting: the autumn volume forecast, which she had put at plus four and which had come in at minus five.

At four she called him into the small room and shut the door with her heel. "The reduction is one position," she said.

"Mine."

"Yes."

She had rehearsed the next part. What she did instead was take the sheet out of her pocket, unfold it, and turn it round on the table so it faced him. "The severance is calculated on the autumn forecast. That's my number. It's wrong by nine points, and I knew it was wrong in August."

Ellis looked at the sheet without touching it. "Why are you telling me?"

She straightened the edge of it against the table, once. Through the glass the floor was taking calls without her. "Because if you take that letter to HR as it stands, you'll be arguing about a mistake I made and didn't declare. This way you're arguing with the number."

"That's not what I asked."

"No," she said.

He folded the sheet and put it in his jacket, and she watched him do it and did not ask for it back.

## The three-axis card

| Axis | Strong — Nadia | Weak — Nadia |
|---|---|---|
| **Want** | Keep her forecast error off the regional director's desk. Blockable by Ellis, by HR, by the calendar. Visible in allocation: the sheet is carried, folded, re-read at the platform — attention spent on concealment rather than on the conversation. | Nothing that can be blocked. "Do the reduction properly" is a task, not a want; it takes no resources from anything else in her life. |
| **Need** | To be wrong in front of someone and stay in the room. Offered at the moment she could take it — Ellis asks a direct question — and she takes it at a price. | Absent. Nothing is offered, so nothing can be declined; the arrival test has no scene to run in. |
| **Lie** | *A mistake only becomes real when someone else sees it.* Arranged, not asserted: she carries the evidence of the error and rehearses a version in which it stays hers. | Absent. She believes nothing that costs her anything, so nothing in the scene can falsify her. |
| **Wound** | Unstated. The sheet folded twice, the platform, the re-fold she skips — the reader infers a prior exposure that the text never names. | Absent. "Feeling the weight of it" is a mood, not a history. |
| **Contradiction** | Performative, carried somatically: she claims the manager's manner — heel on the door, edge straightened — while doing the one thing that destroys her defence. | None. Efficiency and fairness are compatible; the scene therefore has no room in which two goods can collide. |
| **Cost** | Documented error, a witness who keeps the document, and a floor that watched her shut the door. She cannot ask for the sheet back, so the cost stays in circulation. | A feeling, granted and then withdrawn. Nothing is spent, so nothing is owed. |

## Why it works

1. **The want is priced in attention before it is named.** The sheet is folded twice, carried to a platform, read while the train comes in and put back unfolded wrong. That is allocation: a want which costs composure, and breaks a habit in one sentence, is real; a want asserted in one sentence is a hobby.
2. **The cause and the reason are separated.** The formal reason is "the reduction is one position". The cause is a forecast nine points wrong, in her own hand, known since August. The reader gets both and must decide which one the scene is about.
3. **The lie is arranged rather than stated.** "She had rehearsed the next part" is the whole belief in action: rehearse the clean version until the other one stops being available. Nobody says *she hid it because hiding feels safe*.
4. **The turn is a choice against her interest.** Telling Ellis costs her the defence she spent the morning protecting. A choice that preserves everything is administration; this one forecloses an option, which is what makes it a turn.
5. **The contradiction is performative and costs her.** She keeps the manager's body — heel, straightened edge, "No," she said — while breaking the manager's contract. The manner and the act disagree, and the disagreement is the character.
6. **The need is proven by the arrival test.** "Why are you telling me?" is the offer: she can give the rehearsed answer and keep her cover. She takes the harder one, and then cannot hold it — she does not ask for the sheet back.
7. **The cost is left in the world.** The document is in Ellis's jacket. Nothing is resolved by his reaction, which is withheld; the scene ends on her restraint, not on his gratitude.
8. **No line assigns the meaning.** "That's not what I asked" and "No" leave the reader to name what she has done. The scene's last action is a refusal to act, which is legible without being glossed.

## What not to conclude

- **That every management scene needs a concealed error.** The mechanism is cost, not conspiracy. A version where she simply tells him the truth in the first beat and loses nothing is a worse scene for a different reason: no price.
- **That withholding equals depth.** Nadia withholds one fact for one scene. A character who withholds everything is not deep, she is a puzzle, and the reader stops doing the arithmetic.
- **That the cost must be career-ending.** The price here is a documented error and a witness. Catastrophe is not required; circulation is. A cost nobody can spend later is a fee the author paid on the character's behalf.
- **That the weak version is badly written.** It is clean, and that is the point: the sentences are not the problem. The problem is that the scene cannot be entered by inference.
- **That interiority should be deleted.** The strong version keeps two interior judgements ("which was unlike her", "she had rehearsed"). What it removes is interiority that *replaces* an action.

## Transfer rule

Give the character a want that takes resources, a need that is offered and refused, and one choice that costs her the defence she built in the first paragraph; then let the last action be a refusal to take something back.
