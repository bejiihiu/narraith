# One loan, three surfaces: direct, unloaded subtext, loaded subtext

> Purpose: show the difference between a scene that discusses the thing, one that circles it, and one where circling it costs the characters something visible.
> Read when: two characters need something from each other and you are deciding whether they may say it; also when a subtext scene is competent and inert.
> Related: `methodologies/02-character-and-dialogue.md` — E9, the agenda/surface/failed-bid procedure these versions are built on; `references/character-intelligence.md` — want, need, and what a character will not ask for; `checklists/dialogue.md` — the pass/fail gate.

## What this example tests

Whether "subtext" is being used as a synonym for "withholding". Three versions of one visit: the loan discussed openly, the loan displaced onto a radiator with the balance where it started, and the radiator conversation plus a failed bid, a small cruelty, and an offer that arrives before the request. The third is not better because it is more indirect. It is better because the balance of power changes on the page.

## Setup

June, thirty-eight, drives to her mother's house on a Sunday to ask for £900 against a rent shortfall. She has rehearsed it in the car for a week. Vera's front-room radiator has been leaking into a folded towel since Thursday, and the plumber is coming on Tuesday.

## Version A

*Direct: the loan is the subject, both say what they want, and the terms are the drama.*

"How much?" Vera said.

"Nine hundred."

Vera took the envelope from the telephone table and wrote it down. "Nine hundred. And when would I see it again?"

"March. The flat's on a rolling thing now, so it's not — " June stopped. "March."

"March is four months."

"I know how many months it is."

Vera put the pen down. "Is he paying?"

"That's not — this isn't about him."

"I'm asking what I'm getting into. If I'm lending you nine hundred pounds I'd like to know whether there's another income in the house."

"There isn't. There hasn't been since March."

"All right." Vera opened the drawer under the telephone and took out her bank card and set it on the table between them. "Give me the number and I'll do it now. And I'd like it back in March, June, because I'm not a bank and I don't want to have to ask."

"You won't have to ask."

"Good," Vera said. "Then we won't have to talk about it again."

June read out the account number. The transfer went through while they were both looking at the phone, and then Vera said the plumber was coming Tuesday, and June said she hoped it wasn't the valve again.

## Version B

*Unloaded subtext: the radiator is the only subject; the loan is never named and is the only other thing in the room.*

"It's the valve," June said. She was kneeling on the carpet with her hand held near the pipe, not touching it. "You can hear it."

"I can hear it. I've been hearing it since Thursday." Vera stood in the doorway with her arms folded. "The man's coming Tuesday. Ninety pounds before he's even looked at it. That's the call-out."

"Ninety's not bad."

"Ninety is ninety." Vera looked at the towel. "It was the valve in 2019 and it was the valve in 2015. One day it'll be the whole system and then we'll see what ninety pounds buys."

"It might just be the bleed valve. You could do that yourself with a key."

"I'm not doing it myself." A pause. "Do you want tea?"

"I could be here Tuesday," June said. "If you didn't want to sit in for him. I could take the morning."

"You've got work."

"I could take the morning."

"There's no need," Vera said. "I'll be here. It's my house."

June stood up and brushed the carpet off her knees. "Right."

"Drive careful," Vera said, from the doorway, and June said she would, and the towel stayed where it was under the pipe, dark at one corner and going darker.

## Version C

*Loaded subtext: the same surface, plus a failed bid, a small cruelty, and an offer that takes the ask away from her.*

"It's the valve," June said, kneeling. "You can hear it from here."

"I can hear it. The man's coming Tuesday. Ninety pounds before he's looked at it."

"I could be here Tuesday. I could take the morning."

"You've got work." Vera came and stood over her. "And you always leave a thing till it's stopped working. You did it with the car. You did it with the flat."

June took her hand away from the pipe. "The thing is — "

"There's money in the building society," Vera said. "If it's the rent."

"It's not the rent."

"All right." Vera went back to the doorway and stood with one hand on the frame. "It's not the rent. Tuesday, then. I'll get the man in and he can tell me what it wants."

"I'll come."

"No need," Vera said. "I'll be here." She looked at the towel under the pipe, then at her daughter. "You'll stay for your dinner. There's a bit of the lamb."

"I can't."

"There's a bit of the lamb," Vera said again, "and it won't eat itself," and June sat down on the arm of the sofa with her coat still on, and the radiator ticked, and neither of them said the number.

## Which channel for which scene

| Version | Channel | Surface subject | Actual agenda | What changes | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Direct | The loan and its terms | The same as the surface: June wants £900, Vera wants repayment and a reason | The money moves, the rent is covered, and the relationship is unchanged | No second layer: the real currency — who is allowed to need — is untouched; the scene is a transaction, and if the story is about the relationship it has said nothing |
| B | Oblique, unloaded | The leaking radiator and the plumber's £90 | June wants £900 without asking; Vera wants to be needed and will not name the need either | June's offer of help is refused, so the ask becomes impossible; the pattern is confirmed and the visit ends with the need still unspoken | Needs the reader to know why she came, or the radiator is only a radiator; the change is a foreclosed possibility, which can read as stasis in a plot-driven book |
| C | Oblique, loaded | The same radiator | The same two agendas, plus Vera's need to be the one who names it | Vera names the need, so June loses the ask and must accept a gift or refuse one; the cruelty goes unpaid; the balance moves to Vera and stays there | The mother tips into villainy if the cruelty has no interior logic; the offer needs B's pressure behind it or it lands as a trick rather than a decision |

1. **Measure the gap first.** If the surface subject and the agenda are the same thing, you are writing a transaction scene. That is a legitimate form — a negotiation, a briefing, a confession — but do not call it subtext and do not add fog to it.
2. **The surface must be worth the minutes.** A radiator, a parking fee, a sister's operation: something literal that two people would actually discuss. If the surface is a metaphor for the real subject, the scene becomes allegory and the reader stops believing the people.
3. **Unloaded subtext needs a failed bid, or nothing was wanted.** In B, "I could take the morning" reaches and is refused. Without it the scene is two people being polite about a pipe, and the reader has no evidence of cost.
4. **Loaded subtext needs a shift in the balance — specifically, a change in who is allowed to name the thing.** The mother offering before the ask is not a kindness that resolves the scene; it is a move that takes the request away and makes the daughter a recipient. That is the load.
5. **A cruelty must be defensible from inside the speaker.** "You always leave a thing till it's stopped working" is a line about a radiator that is also about a marriage, and Vera can believe it is advice. If she cannot, it is a villain line and the scene flattens.
6. **Directness is the right answer where transparency is the effect.** Confession, briefing, dying declaration, formal interview, and any scene where the terms themselves are the drama. A is not a weak version of C; it is the right version of a different scene.

## What not to conclude

- **Not "direct dialogue is weak."** A is the correct scene for a story about a transaction, a family that says things, or a mother whose bluntness is the point. E9 lists the cases where blurring directness destroys the scene.
- **Not "subtext means never naming the thing."** C names it — the rent, the money, the number — and is still loaded, because the subtext has moved from *what is said* to *who says it and when*. That is the harder and more useful lesson.
- **Not "more indirection is better."** B withholds more than C in one sense and is the weaker scene, because nothing in the balance moves. Load is a change, not a quantity of silence.
- **Not "the mother is the villain."** Her offer has to be a real attempt to help that also costs her daughter something. Both must be true at once, or the scene is a moral cartoon.
- **Not "cut the stated feeling and the plot."** The reader needs the setup — the £900, the rent, the rehearsed week — or B and C become a private joke between author and page.

## Transfer rule

Subtext is a second layer built on a first layer that is genuinely about something; if the surface subject could be swapped for any other pretext without loss, there is no subtext, only avoidance.
