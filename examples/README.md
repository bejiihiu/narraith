# Examples — ten worked cases

> Purpose: index the worked pairs so a diagnosis can be checked against a case instead of against a principle.
> Read when: you have named a defect and want to see it repaired at the right level, or you are calibrating your reading before you critique a draft.
> Related: `SKILL.md` — the seven levels and the scene gate every pair is judged by; `references/quality-levels.md` — the grid the diagnosis tables use; `references/editorial-intelligence.md` — the repair order the strong versions follow.

## The shape of a pair

Every file runs one route: a setup stating only what the prose needs; a weak version marked **Weak.**; a diagnosis table with symptom, level, mechanism and quoted evidence; a strong version marked **Strong.**; a numbered account of why it works; a short list of what not to conclude; and one transfer rule.

The weak version is never a strawman. It is the best version of the wrong decision — clean sentences, legible feeling, no obvious error. That is the point: if the weak version were badly written, the pair would teach line editing, and the defect in all ten is upstream of the line.

Two notes on the table. Pairs 07 and 08 carry no level tags on purpose: both argue their decision is a question of scene function, and 07 argues explicitly against reading the three emotion channels as a ranking. And the levels column gives the level the *diagnosis* owns, which is often not the level the symptom appears at — 06 reads as a plot problem and is owned by composition.

## How to use these examples

1. **Read the weak version first and diagnose it yourself.** Write your own table before you look at theirs. The value of the pair is the gap between your diagnosis and the file's, and that gap disappears the moment you read the answer key.
2. **Compare decisions before sentences.** Ask what each version decided — who acts, what it costs, what the reader is told and when — and only then look at the prose. Two versions can differ in every sentence and decide the same thing.
3. **Do not copy sentences.** The strong versions are written to one premise and one voice; lifted lines will not survive in your story. The mechanisms will.
4. **The transfer rule is the only portable part.** Everything above it is evidence for the rule, and the rule is a sentence you can apply to a draft that shares nothing with the example.
5. **Use the level column to route.** If your diagnosis names a different level than the file, one of you has misassigned the owner — check which with the level interaction map in `references/quality-levels.md` before you revise anything.
6. **Take the mechanism, not the setting.** The pairs are contemporary, mostly British, mostly domestic. A management scene, a village in 1919 and a tyre yard are three costumes for the same question: what does the decision cost, and who pays.

## Reading order

- **First calibration:** 01, then 03, then 09. A scene, a character and a narrator — the same question at three scales: what is at stake, who pays, and who is telling.
- **Revision work:** 05, then 04, then 06. What the reader is told, when they are told it, and whether the ending re-prices what they already hold.
- **Dialogue:** 02, then 08. Positions first, then agendas; a scene cannot have subtext until each speaker wants something they will not ask for.
- **Reflex checks:** 07 and 10 are the two pairs whose weak version is not a defect but a default — the body because the rule says to show, and the arc because the premise arrived with one.

## Coverage and gaps

- The ten concentrate on L4–L7, because that is where the decisions live that the rest of the skill assumes. L1 and L2 appear mainly as symptoms of an upstream problem (05, 09) rather than as subjects.
- There is no novel-scale pair. Everything here is a scene, a story or a story plan; long-form work is covered by `workflows/02-long-form.md` and `references/story-architecture.md`, without a worked pair.
- There is no Russian-language pair. Example 01 carries a Russian syntactic variant and `references/russian-prose.md` covers the language layer; the gap is real and should be closed before the skill is used for Russian prose at length.
- Nothing here tests genre. Dread, crime and romance have their own promises, and refusing them is not originality — see `references/genre-and-form.md`.

## What these examples are not

- **Not a style guide.** No sentence in the strong versions is offered as a model of good prose. Several are deliberately plain; one or two are deliberately odd.
- **Not a corpus.** Ten pairs are ten decisions, not a distribution. Nothing here supports a claim about how fiction is usually written.
- **Not evidence that the skill works.** These are demonstrations of a procedure applied by hand. Whether the procedure improves a draft is an empirical question, and the honest answer lives in `tests/` and `docs/limitations.md`.
- **Not a template for premises.** The setups are ordinary on purpose. A premise borrowed from here will produce a story that has already been written.
- **Not a detector.** Nothing in these files identifies a machine-written text, and no pair should be read as a signature to match against.
