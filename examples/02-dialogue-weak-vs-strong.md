# Dialogue: dismantling the deck

> Purpose: show an exchange that carries a promotion, a grievance and a refusal without either speaker naming any of them, against a version where everyone says exactly what they feel.
> Read when: dialogue reads as a transcript of positions; when two characters are emotionally articulate and the scene is dead; when you are about to add subtext without first giving each speaker an agenda.
> Related: `methodologies/02-character-and-dialogue.md` — E9, the subtext-move table and the worked exchange this file is calibrated against; `checklists/dialogue.md` — the twelve checks; `references/character-intelligence.md` — want, need and defence; `references/quality-levels.md` — L4 and L6.

## What this example tests

Whether an exchange can carry a promotion, a grievance and a refusal while neither speaker says what they want. The weak version fails at L4/E9: every line states a position, so the subtext is already text and the reader has nothing to assemble. The strong version keeps the quarrel inside a task that has its own rules, and the shift is measured in territory rather than in feeling.

## Setup

Marta and Owen have run the same account for four years. The quarterly review ended twenty minutes ago, and Owen's promotion was announced in it. The room is booked until six; the printed deck is confidential and goes to the locked bins on four.

## Weak version

**Weak.**

"You could have said something in there," Marta said.

"About what?"

"About Berlin. About whose numbers those were."

Owen sighed and began stacking the printouts. "It wasn't my decision, Marta. You know it wasn't."

"That's what everyone says."

"I didn't ask for it."

"You didn't turn it down either."

He stopped stacking. "Do you think I wanted this? Do you think I don't know what it looks like?"

"I think you've wanted it for two years. I think you've been waiting for me to be fine with it so you could stop feeling guilty."

"That's not fair."

"None of it is fair." She felt the anger arrive in her chest and pressed her hand flat against the table. "I've carried this account for four years. Four years, and they gave it to you because you're better at the meetings."

"Marta—"

"Don't." She stacked the papers into a neat pile. "I'm not going to pretend I'm happy for you. I'm not happy for you."

"I never asked you to be happy. I asked you to be professional."

"Then I'll be professional." She picked up her bag. "See you Monday."

The room was very quiet. Owen went on stacking the printouts.

## Diagnosis

| Symptom | Level | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grievances are exchanged as positions | L4 / E9 | **Missing move: deflection.** Nothing has to be evaded because every complaint is delivered whole; each line rebuts the previous claim, so the exchange is a rally rather than a negotiation. | "I've carried this account for four years." |
| Feeling is named at the point of pressure | L4 | **Missing move: category shift.** The emotional category is answered in the emotional category. No one replies with a date, a number or a procedure, so nothing is refused and nothing has to be inferred. | "She felt the anger arrive in her chest" |
| One character diagnoses the other | L6 / E9 | **Missing move: over-specificity.** No unnecessary precise detail stands in for the accusation; the psychology arrives as a summary, which converts a person into a case with a known cause. | "you've wanted it for two years" |
| The task is a prop, not a surface | L5 / E8 | **Missing move: logistics.** The printouts have no rules, deadline or consequence, so the physical business cannot carry pressure or mark who controls what. | "began stacking the printouts" |
| Every reach is also a rebuttal | L6 / E9 | **Missing move: silence.** Nothing is left unanswered long enough to become a refusal. His bid for sympathy arrives as a counter-claim, so no one can lose anything by reaching. | "Do you think I wanted this?" |
| The rupture is announced, and the scene closes on a mood | L2 / L5 | **Missing move: misnaming.** Nobody renames the relation or the possession — no "your deck", no "your team" — so the new hierarchy is reported by the last line instead of transacted in address. | "The room was very quiet." |

## Strong version

**Strong.**

"Leave the Berlin slide up," Owen said. "I want a photograph of it for Monday."

Marta kept unclipping. "It's the wrong number. It was wrong in March."

"I know it's wrong."

"Then don't photograph it." She laid the sheet face down and squared the pile with both hands.

He came round the table, took the marker out of the tray, capped it, and set it down again. "The session ran over because of the questions. That's not the deck's fault."

"Nothing's the deck's fault."

"HR said the paperwork goes through on the first."

"Which first."

"The first of the month."

"Then I need the Q3 headcount by the twenty-eighth. The template locks on the twenty-ninth, and I'm not doing it by hand again."

Owen looked at the pile. "You had the Milan numbers before I did. In February. I'd like that in the room on Monday."

"The room's booked till six."

"I'm not asking about the room."

"No." She unclipped the last sheet. "You're not."

He picked up the clicker, turned it over, put it down. In the corridor a trolley went past, and a door took a long time to close.

"You should take the Singapore section," he said.

"It's in your deck."

"It was in the plan. Before."

Marta lifted the Singapore sheets off the pile, squared them, and slid them into her bag. Owen watched her do it and said nothing.

"I'll tell them you're doing it," he said.

"Tell them whatever you like." She took the rest of the pile and left the clicker on the table. At the door she stopped. "The bins on four are locked after six."

"I know," he said. "I'll take them down."

## Why it works

1. **"Leave the Berlin slide up."** — the first line is a production note and a claim on her at once: he needs her for Monday, and he opens by handing her an instruction she did not ask for. The agenda arrives dressed as logistics, so the scene is already negotiating before either of them admits anything (E9: agenda by indirection).
2. **"It's the wrong number. It was wrong in March."** — over-specificity as reproach. The date is the argument and it is factually correct, so she cannot be accused of making it personal.
3. **"took the marker out of the tray, capped it, and set it down again"** — silence as a move. The objects absorb what he will not say, and the fiddling is visible without a stage direction or an adverb.
4. **"the paperwork goes through on the first" / "Which first."** — the promotion enters as an administrative date. He will not say the word; she refuses the emotional frame by demanding a calendar. The reader assembles both halves (category shift).
5. **"You had the Milan numbers before I did. In February."** — the failed bid. It is an offer of shared history and continued partnership, disguised as a compliment about her competence, and it costs him something to say.
6. **"The room's booked till six."** — deflection that returns his own word, *room*, as a booking. The bid is refused without ever being acknowledged, which leaves him no way to insist.
7. **"No." … "You're not."** — the one place she answers the act instead of the line, and she still does not name what he is asking. It is a refusal, not an explanation, so the reader keeps the inference.
8. **"It's in your deck." / "It was in the plan. Before."** — misnaming as a claim about possession. The possessive is the whole argument. Then she takes the sheets and he watches: the balance moves through an object, not a statement.
9. **"The bins on four are locked after six." / "I'll take them down."** — the last inversion. The promoted man accepts the confidential waste, and the new order is acknowledged by both without either naming it.

## What not to conclude

- **Not "dialogue must never state anything."** Directness is a choice where transparency is the effect — a confession, a briefing, a formal warning. The failure here is that directness is the default, not that it exists.
- **Not "make everyone oblique."** Obliqueness without agendas is absence, not subtext. If neither of them wants anything, the surface has nothing to conceal.
- **Not "the promoted character must be a villain."** Owen is generous and evasive; a cruel Owen would let Marta's refusal cost nothing, and the scene would become a punishment instead of a negotiation.
- **Not "every exchange needs a failed bid."** One per scene. A second bid turns the exchange into a plea, and the reader stops believing either of them has work to do.
- **Not "end on a reversal."** The shift is small and deniable — a section of a deck, a bag of waste. A reversal would be a different story, and a larger one.

## Transfer rule

Give two people a shared task with rules they both respect, let each want something the task cannot give, and end when one of them takes a piece of the task as payment.
