# Detector report — 2026-11-23

> Purpose: record what a commercial detector said about a story written under v1.0.0, and what the skill changed as a result.
> Read when: reviewing the detector-feedback module, or deciding whether a flag is a craft defect.

**Document checked:** a Russian short story, 884 words, 5,627 characters, written under Narraith 1.0.0
(a server administrator, one evening, a plugin logging every purchase).

**Detector:** Textovod, report dated 23.11.2026 22:36 MSK.
**Verdict:** machine generation detected. **57%** explicit AI signs, **43%** suspicious fragments.
**Vendor's own claim on the page:** 99.3% recall on generated texts, 98.9% specificity on human texts.
That claim is about the vendor's benchmark and was not independently checked here.

## The fragments

The PDF marks spans in colour. Colour is lost in text extraction, so fragment-level attribution was not
possible from the artifact. This is a limitation of the report format, not of the detector: the online
result shows the spans. Ask for the HTML result when fragment detail matters.

## Diagnosis

The flag coincides with two failures of the skill's own Russian checks:

- **"Long sentences exist."** The checklist asks for at least one periodic sentence per page or two; the
  text has none. A page of short declaratives is described there as reading "as translated or as a report".
- **"Pronouns dropped."** The check operates at sentence level; at paragraph level, more than a fifth of
  paragraphs open on *он*. Technically the pronoun is legal at a paragraph start, but seven of thirty-two
  is a metronome, not a decision.

The prose was not purple, abstract or sentimental. It was **uniform**: same sentence length, same opening
position, same paragraph size. That uniformity is what a classifier trained on generated text is likely
to see.

## What this does not show

- That the story is bad. The rubric scores craft, and craft was not what failed here; this text was never
  judged, and a detector flag is not a quality verdict.
- That the detector is correct about authorship. It measures typicality, not origin.
- That the skill should be optimised for this detector.

## Changes made (v1.1.0)

- New module `references/detector-feedback.md`: the procedure, the four statistical suspects, and the
  prohibition on score-chasing.
- `scripts/story_metrics.py` now reports pronoun-initial share, paragraph-opener share, one-sentence
  paragraph share, paragraph-length spread, and top sentence openers.

