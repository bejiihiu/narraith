Rain had been falling on the old city long enough to cut channels in the stone. It came down the gutters and stood in the low places of the paving, and the quarter smelled of wet lime. Elena walked out into it without an umbrella, and the water found the seams of her coat. She was remembering. Not any one thing — the shape of the years, all of it arriving at once and none of it adding up. At the corner the buildings leaned toward each other and the street narrowed, and she stopped, and let the rain take the warmth off her face. She had stood here before, in other weather. Then she went on up the street, and she knew she would not come back down it.

**What I changed and why**

The draft stated the feeling four times — sadness, a shattering heart, a tidal wave, a mournful echo — so I kept one channel and let the rest be inferred. Both similes were dead (angels' tears, tidal wave); I replaced them with the rain's actual work, water that has worn channels in the stone. That turns the city's age into a fact instead of the adjective "ancient." "Remembered everything that had ever happened to her" became the texture of the remembering rather than its inventory. I kept the closing recognition, but moved it out of abstraction into a concrete thing she knows, and shortened the sentences at each turn so the turns land.
