The rain had been falling on the old city for days. It ran down the rooftops and pooled in the gutters, and the mist it raised softened the edges of everything—the awnings, the shuttered windows, the stone faces above the street.

Elena walked slowly, deliberately, with no destination she could have named. Water had got into her shoes. She was crying, and had been crying long enough that she had stopped noticing, except when she breathed. She remembered everything, and none of it in order. It came at her from the doorways she passed, from the sound of her own steps, and she did not turn to look. There was nothing behind her she could bear to see twice.

She understood then, not as a thought but as a fact she had been avoiding, that nothing would ever be the same again.

---

**Changes:** Cut the stacked adjectives and the three clichéd similes—angel tears, tidal wave, shattered heart—that were announcing emotion instead of producing it. Replaced them with concrete detail: rain in the gutters, water in her shoes, the sound of her steps. Kept all four beats (rain, memory, walk, realization), the close third person, and the final certainty. Memory is now rendered as something arriving unbidden rather than as the vague "everything that had ever happened to her." Varied sentence length so the prose breathes, and let the last line land as consequence rather than declaration.
