# Workflows: fourteen routes

> Purpose: pick the route, the artifacts, the gate and the repair loop for the request in front of you.
> Read when: a request names a length, a unit, a revision, a voice or a structure, and before the first sentence is written.
> Related: `../SKILL.md` — tiers, the scene gate, revision triage. `../references/editorial-intelligence.md` — repair patterns. `../references/literary-critique.md` — diagnosis when the user asks what is wrong, not for a fix.

Engines answer *how to make one decision well*. Workflows answer *in what order, for this request, at
what resolution*. A route that lists steps without naming what each step produces is a table of contents;
every route here names its artifacts, its gate, and the loop that runs when the gate fails.

The engine column lists what a route may need, not what it must run. A story uses three or four engines, and a
route that runs all of its listed engines has satisfied a checklist instead of making a decision (`../SKILL.md`).

## Routing rule

If the request is **X** and the material is **Y**, start here:

| X — the request | Y — what exists | Start | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| "write me a story" | nothing, under 7,500 words | W1 | `../references/story-architecture.md` if the turn is unearned |
| "a novella" | nothing, 7,500–40,000 | W2 | W1's stages, plus the subplot audit |
| "a novel" | a premise, a cast, or a first act | W3 | W4 per chapter, once the spine exists |
| "chapter nine" | chapters one to eight | W4 | continuity read before the first sentence, no exceptions |
| "this scene is flat" | a scene | W5 | W6 if the flatness is all in the talk |
| "make them sound different" | dialogue | W6 | W7 if the problem is that they want the same thing |
| "she feels generic" | a draft or a plan | W7 | W5 to test the fix in a scene |
| "build me a world" | a premise | W8 | W3 if the world is carrying a book |
| "continue this" | someone else's text | W9 | continuity extraction first, always |
| "this is weak" | one unit under ~5,000 words | W10 | W11 if the unit is part of a manuscript |
| "edit my book" | 20,000+ words | W11 | W10 for any unit the plan marks as rewrite |
| "write like this" | a sample of 300+ words | W12 | W1 or W5 inside the acquired voice |
| "tell it out of order" | a story or a spine | W13 | on top of W1–W3, never instead of them |
| "three plot lines" | a spine or a draft | W14 | on top of W3 |
| "why doesn't this work?" | any text | W10 stages 1–2, then stop | `../references/literary-critique.md`, then a route for the repair |

Two rules override the table. **An existing text always gets a continuity read before a plan** — a plan
built on a misremembered fact is wasted work. **A request to diagnose is not a request to write**: answer
with the defect and the level, then ask for the repair only if the user wants it.

## W12–W14 modify; they do not replace

These three are structural modes, not standalone lengths. A non-linear short story still needs W1's cost
and scene cards; W13 changes the order of delivery and adds the anchor and reconstruction tests. A novel
with three lines still needs W3's spine; W14 adds line sheets, intersections and POV allocation. Entering
W13 or W14 alone produces structure without a story — the most common and most expensive misroute.

## Workflow theatre, and how to collapse it

The method has a failure mode of its own: performing the process instead of writing. Symptoms, all
recognisable in the first minute:

- a ledger, a spine or a chapter map produced for a request that asked for a scene;
- a plan longer than the text it plans (the rule: over one page of plan for under 2,000 words is a defect);
- questions to the user that could have been inferred and named as assumptions;
- four revision passes on 800 words;
- stage names, level tags and engine IDs in the reply, when the user asked for a story;
- refusing to start because a decision is unresolved — the decision can be drafted and tested.

The tier in `../SKILL.md` sets the number of stages. **Tier A**: no route. **Tier B**: three stages
(decide, write, gate plus one pass). **Tier C**: the full route. Within a route, pick the two stages that
carry this request's failure risk and skip the rest silently; the artifact is scaffolding, and scaffolding
comes down. A gate that passes without effort is not testing the thing that fails: if every scene passes
"state change" in one word, you are answering the question you can answer, not the one that matters.

## Shared invariants

1. Every route ends with a state change that can be stated in one sentence, with a before and an after.
2. Every repair loop is ordered by leverage, not by ease: structure, then scene, then paragraph, then line.
3. Every route reports its assumptions in one line, and none of them asks the user to fill in a form.
4. No route promises measurement. `../scripts/story_metrics.py` computes surface proxies and says so.

## Artifact names

Canonical, and not to be renamed: **NDL / Decision Ledger** (`../templates/decision-ledger.md`), **Voice
Contract**, **Information Ledger**, **Continuity Ledger** (`../templates/continuity-ledger.md`), **Scene
Card**, **Revision Plan**. A route may use a subset. A route may not invent a seventh artifact and call
it canonical.
