# Decision Ledger

> Purpose: fix the load-bearing narrative decisions before prose, so that each scene executes a decision instead of improvising one.
> Use when: planning anything longer than a scene, or diagnosing a draft that reads competently and means nothing.
> Pairs with: `templates/scene-card.md` — scenes are where ledger nodes become prose. `templates/voice-contract.md` — the voice fields live there. `references/story-architecture.md` — the reasoning behind each field.

```
KIND
  [attitudinal | situational | narrative | expository] — and why not narrative by default:

DRAMATIC QUESTION
  [one question the reader wants answered, unanswerable until the end]:

PRESSURE
  the force:
  what it wants:
  why it is right from inside itself:
  why it cannot simply be removed:

PROTAGONIST
  want (pursued, concrete, visible in behaviour):
  need (required, unadmitted):
  lie (the false belief that makes the pursuit costly):
  cost of acting:
  cost of succeeding:

ANTAGONIST
  the true thing they are defending:
  what they are wrong about:
  what they would have to lose to give way:

CAUSAL SPINE — seven nodes, each caused by the one before
  1  the situation that is already unstable:
  2  the inciting choice (a choice, not an event):
  3  the consequence that closes the exit:
  4  the reversal of understanding:
  5  the cost made visible:
  6  the choice that cannot be unmade:
  7  the new equilibrium, and what it cost:

TURNING POINTS — three at most
  [what changes] · [who chooses] · [what it costs them]

INFORMATION PLAN
  reader learns 1:
  reader learns 2:
  reader learns 3:
  protagonist learns:
  what keeps each gap open:

TEMPORAL PLAN
  order:
  what the order buys that chronology would not:
  anchors:
  elapsed time, start to end:

SUBPLOTS
  [name] — its own question — where it intersects the spine — what it costs the main line:

ENDING MODE
  [resolved | ambiguous | ironic | circular | open | tragic] — why this mode for this question:

MORAL CENTRE
  the judgement the work makes without ever stating it:

MOTIFS
  [object / gesture / word] — first appearance — what it means by the end:

REFUSALS — the originality mechanism
  1  default solution rejected:            replaced by:
  2  default solution rejected:            replaced by:
  3  default solution rejected:            replaced by:
```

**Minimum viable ledger — nine fields, for a short story or a single chapter.** The full ledger is for work that will be revised against itself; below that, it produces documents instead of stories. (This is a different list from the eight decisions in `SKILL.md`: those are made in the head before any note, and this is the smallest note worth keeping.)

```
1  kind
2  dramatic question
3  want / need / lie
4  pressure, plus its good-faith argument
5  cost of acting and cost of succeeding
6  information plan: three revelations, in order
7  time / order
8  ending mode
9  two refusals
```

**E11 receipt — one per tested decision.** The refusals above are the plan; this is the audit trail for a
single turning point, scene, or sentence. Keep it in the working note, not the ledger, unless the decision
is load-bearing.

```
UNIT          [scene / turn / sentence]
MODAL         the version a competent re-writer would produce, one line
ALTERNATIVES  three to five, each with its declared axis
CHOSEN        which one, and the axis it came from
PREPARATION   the earlier material that makes it available — or "none", which fails
COST          what it costs, and to whom
VERDICT       prepared and non-modal / prepared but modal, kept deliberately / arbitrary
ROUND         1 or 2, then stop
```

How to use: fill it once, in one sitting, badly. The value is in the collision between fields — a want that contradicts the cost, a lie that the ending mode cannot resolve, an antagonist whose argument is stronger than the protagonist's. Those collisions are the work; a tidy ledger is usually a ledger that has not been thought through.

When to stop: when a field starts generating prose instead of constraining it, stop filling and write. Come back only if a scene fails the gate for a reason you cannot name. A ledger revised after every chapter is a way of not writing.
