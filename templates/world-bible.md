# World Bible

> Purpose: record only the parts of a world that constrain a character's choices, and prove in one page that the world is doing work rather than providing weather.
> Use when: the setting is invented, altered, or so specific that its rules decide what a scene can contain.
> Pairs with: `templates/decision-ledger.md` — the world's pressure enters there as the antagonist's argument. `templates/character-bible.md` — what a person costs inside these rules.

```
RULES
  physical     [what the body can and cannot do here]:
  social       [who may do what to whom, and the penalty]:
  technological [what exists, what does not, who can afford it]:
  magical      [if any: the cost, the limit, and who pays it]
  the rule the plot will test first:

WHAT IS NORMAL
  [three things nobody in this world remarks on, which a reader would]:

WHAT IS SCARCE
  [the thing that makes the plot's economy work — water, time, trust, labour, paper]:

WHO HAS POWER
  [office] holds [what] by [what means] — and who can take it from them:

WHAT EVERYONE BELIEVES THAT IS FALSE
  the belief:
  who benefits from it:
  who knows better, and what it costs them to say so:

SENSORY SIGNATURE
  what this world smells like:
  what it sounds like at the hour the story keeps returning to:
  the one texture a character touches without thinking:

LOCATIONS — three, each with a social meaning
  [place]  physical fact:               what it costs to be seen here:
  [place]  physical fact:               what it costs to be seen here:
  [place]  physical fact:               what it costs to be seen here:
```

**The one-page test.** In 150 words or fewer, state three decisions the world's rules force on a character who would rather not make them. Not three features of the world — three *choices*, each traceable to a rule above, each costing something the character wanted. Then run the substitution check: if the same three choices would survive in a generic version of this setting, the world is not doing work and the rules above are decoration.

How to use: fill the rules only as far as the current chapter needs them, and add a rule when a scene forces one. A world bible written ahead of the prose invents constraints nobody will use, and the unused constraints are what make invented worlds feel like encyclopedias.

When to stop: when a new entry no longer changes what a character can do. Cosmology, dynasties and languages are not constraints until someone has to choose inside them — and the reader will supply more world from three good sensory details than from three pages of history.
