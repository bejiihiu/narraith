# Scene Card

> Purpose: decide a scene's change, cost and information before writing it, in less time than it takes to write the first paragraph.
> Use when: drafting any scene that carries plot, or diagnosing one that reads well and does nothing.
> Pairs with: `templates/decision-ledger.md` — which node this scene executes. `references/continuity-memory.md` §4 — why the clock field exists.

```
SCENE   [working name]
  POV         whose head, and why this head for this scene:
  place       [where, and what the room is doing to them]:
  time        clock starts:            runs for:
              story position: [A2 + 3 days]   chapter:

  QUESTION    what the POV is trying to get, in one sentence:
  ENTRY STATE [knowledge / trust / power / possibility — before]:
  EXIT STATE  [the same four — after]: at least one must differ

  WHAT CHANGES   [the difference, stated as a fact a stranger could check]:

  WHO CHOOSES    [character] chooses [specific act]
  AT WHAT COST   [what it costs, and who pays]

  INFORMATION
    given to the reader:
    withheld, and on what the withholding rests:
    the detail the reader will remember:

  THE TURN    [the moment the scene reverses, and what causes it]:
  LAST LINE   [the line that lands on the change — write it now, or accept
               that the scene has no ending yet]
```

How to use: write entry and exit states before the scene, the last line after it. The one field that must not be left blank is *at what cost* — a scene whose turn costs nobody anything has produced information, not drama, and information alone is an errand. If the turn is caused by an arrival, a message or an accident, note it: that is the diagnostic for an unearned scene, not a detail to fix in the prose.

When to stop: a card that takes longer than the scene is a substitute for it. Three lines on a sticky note — question, change, cost — will do for a scene that is not load-bearing. Not every scene gets a card; the ones that carry a turning point do.
