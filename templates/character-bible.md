# Character Bible

> Purpose: hold one character's pressure system in the form a scene can use — want, lie, contradiction, voice — without turning them into a description.
> Use when: casting a work with more than two people, or when a character is well described and inert.
> Pairs with: `templates/continuity-ledger.md` — the checkable values (age, body, spellings). `references/character-intelligence.md` — the reasoning behind each field. `templates/voice-contract.md` — for narrators, which is a different job.

```
NAME / ROLE
  [name] · [function in the story, not their job title] · [age, birth year]

IDENTITY — three facts a stranger could verify
  1:
  2:
  3:

WANT          [pursued now, concrete, visible in behaviour]:
NEED          [required, unadmitted, and why admitting it would cost them]:
LIE           [the false belief, stated as a sentence they would say]:
  where it came from:
  what it protects them from feeling:

WOUND
  the event:
  what they concluded from it:
  what they do now to prevent a repeat:

CONTRADICTION — pick one kind
  [value conflict | temporal contradiction | performative contradiction | somatic contradiction]
  the two sides:
  the cost, and to whom, in which scene:
  second occurrence, at higher stakes:
  third occurrence, if any:

VOICE
  syntax habit (sentence shape they default to):
  lexical field (six words or images they return to):
  taboo (what they will not say, and what they say instead):
  silence behaviour (what they do when they refuse to answer):

RELATIONSHIPS — one block per significant other
  with [OTHER]:
    initiative — who starts:
    permission — who may ask, touch, refuse, enter:
    debt — owed by whom, in what, mentionable or not:
    taboo — what cannot be raised:
    physical grammar — distance, touch, who handles whose things:

KNOWLEDGE STATE (as of [chapter])
  knows:
  believes:
  wrong about:
  suspects:

PHYSICAL FACTS
  body, marks, injuries and what they restrict:
  what they carry and what they wear when it matters:
  (authoritative values live in the continuity ledger; keep this short)

ARC TYPE
  [reversal | revelation | hardening | refusal]
  what changes, and what it costs, paid to whom:
  same-situation return: [the earlier scene] → [what they do there at the end]

THREE BEHAVIOURS THAT REVEAL THE LIE
  1  [situation] → [what they do]:
  2  [situation] → [what they do]:
  3  [situation] → [what they do]:
  (none of the three may mention the lie; the pattern is the evidence)
```

How to use: fill the top four fields first and stop. If want, need, lie and wound do not generate a contradiction and a voice, the rest of the page will be decoration. The three behaviours are the only section that must be written *after* a draft, because you cannot predict a gesture — you can only notice the one you have already used twice.

When to stop: a bible longer than a page is a novel about the character that will never be written. Cut anything that could not appear in a scene. If a detail is needed for continuity rather than for writing, it belongs in the continuity ledger, where it can be checked instead of admired.
