# Revision Plan

> Purpose: convert a diagnosis into a work order sorted by what breaks if left alone and what the repair costs to make, so that the plan cannot be executed in the wrong order.
> Use when: a draft has been diagnosed and is about to be cut, and whenever the temptation is to start with the sentences.
> Pairs with: `references/literary-critique.md` §Severity and leverage — the bands and the cap. `references/editorial-intelligence.md` — repair patterns per level.

**Deferred** — one line each, no repair yet:

```
[defect]  —  [why it waits: depends on #N / below the cap / not yet diagnosed]
```

**How many defects to include.** Five, not twenty-five. The cap is one fatal or two structural plus three local; everything else goes on the deferred list. Two rules govern it.

- If a **fatal** defect appears, the plan is one row and a stop. Every other row would be built on a premise that is about to change, and a long plan built on a changing premise teaches the writer to distrust plans.
- If more than **twelve** defects survive triage and none of them is fatal, the list is the symptom: the same failure is appearing at several levels, and it should be diagnosed once at the level where it originates. That is a diagnosis problem, not a longer plan.

How to use: fill `evidence` before `repair`, always — a defect with no quote is a taste, and a plan of tastes cannot be argued with or checked. Fill `risk` honestly; it is what tells you whether row 3 invalidates rows 4 and 5, and rows are ordered so this happens as rarely as possible. Then execute top to bottom and do not skip to a lower row because it is easier.

When to stop: when the repairs start generating new defects of the same kind. That is the signal that the band above was misjudged, and revising further converts a plan into churn.
