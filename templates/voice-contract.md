# Voice Contract

> Purpose: define the narrator precisely enough to be reproduced on a bad day, and short enough to be re-read before every session.
> Use when: starting a work, switching narrators between chapters, matching an existing voice, or finding that a draft has drifted into a generic literary register.
> Pairs with: `references/prose-engineering.md` — the mechanics behind each field. `templates/character-bible.md` — voice in dialogue is a different object from voice in narration.

```
POV          [first / second / third limited / third omniscient] · [whose]
DISTANCE     [inside the body | just behind the eyes | across the room | above the scene]
TENSE        [past / present] — and whether it ever moves, and where

REGISTER     [the social register of the narration, not of the characters]:
             [what it is not — the register it would default to]:

ATTENTION PATTERN
  notices first:            [the category of thing this narrator always sees]
  never notices:            [the category it reliably misses, and why that is character]
  describes at length:      [what earns detail]
  summarizes:               [what gets skipped]

SYNTAX SIGNATURE
  default sentence shape:
  what happens to the syntax under stress:
  the move this narrator makes that no one else would:

LEXICAL FIELD
  six words or images it returns to:
  words it would never use:
  the concrete domain it draws comparisons from:

FIGURATION HABIT
  [simile / metaphor / neither] · [how often] · [drawn from what] ·
  [whether the narrator knows they are doing it]:

ATTITUDE
  toward the events:
  toward the reader:
  toward themselves:

REFUSALS — what this voice will not do
  1:
  2:
  3:

OBLIGATIONS — what it must do every chapter
  1:
  2:
  3:

CALIBRATION SAMPLE — exactly 40 words, in voice, about nothing in particular
  "[40 words]"
```

How to use: write the calibration sample last and keep it to 40 words, so that comparing it against a new page takes seconds. Every field above is a claim that can be checked against those 40 words — if the sample does not show the lexical field and the syntax signature, they are aspirations, not a contract. Re-read the sample, not the whole page, when a chapter starts sounding like every other book.

When to stop: when the contract describes a style rather than a person. A voice with no blind spot, no refusal and no verbal tic is a register, and a register can be replaced by any competent imitation — which is exactly the failure the contract exists to prevent. Three refusals are enough; six means you are writing a manifesto.
